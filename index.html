01 / Evaluation tension

Realism is not a scalar.

The project starts with deliberately limited policies so their different failure modes remain legible. These are evaluation hypotheses that M3 will turn into measured dimensions.

≠

Logged similarity is not reactivity.

Perfect trajectory replay can reproduce a recording exactly while ignoring every counterfactual change made by the ego vehicle.

≠

Low collision is not useful motion.

An overly defensive agent may avoid contact by freezing or yielding unnaturally. Safety counts alone cannot explain whether behavior is plausible.

≠

Diversity is not feasibility.

A model can produce varied futures by adding noise—even when those futures leave the road or violate basic kinematics.

03 / One rollout transition

The engine owns the physics boundary.

Policies propose typed state overrides or controls. The engine owns time, identity, lifecycle, feasibility integration, and the evidence trail.

  1. Copy historyPreserve all observations through current_index.
  2. Observe nowBuild immutable current-step frames; CV and IDM are tested against poisoned future world kinematics.
  3. Policy stepReturn typed controls or explicit absolute-state overrides.
  4. IntegrateApply actual Δt and audited acceleration, speed, braking, and yaw clamps.
  5. Update togetherSynchronously advance world agents; preserve births and re-entries.
  6. Record proofEmit rollout state plus complete engine and policy provenance.

One contract, two kinds of actor control

In M2, the ego remains exogenous and follows its logged trajectory. Policies simulate world agents from the current state. M5 can replace ego motion through a typed perturbation without changing the world-policy seam.

04 / Evidence

Tests target the seams, not the screenshots.

The suite uses analytic oracles, poisoned inputs, lifecycle probes, malformed policies, batch acceptance, and exact serialization round-trips.

EVIDENCE / A

Future-log poisoning

CV and IDM receive altered future world kinematics without changing their rollouts, showing that these two policies do not consume those future values.

EVIDENCE / B

Analytic dynamics

Irregular Δt, speed caps, timed stops, no-reverse behavior, and clamp counts are checked against explicit oracles.

EVIDENCE / C

Lifecycle identity

Births, gaps, re-entries, validity masks, synchronous updates, and agent identity remain engine-owned.

EVIDENCE / D

Adversarial boundaries

Malformed policy steps, input mutation, retained buffers, invalid ego indices, and reusable instances are rejected or isolated.

EVIDENCE / E

Exact replay

Log replay reproduces valid recorded agent fields exactly across the 50-scenario synthetic evaluation set.

EVIDENCE / F

IDM equation

Canonical acceleration, leader selection, bumper gaps, monotonicity, fallback behavior, and clamps have direct checks.

EVIDENCE / G

Batch acceptance

50 scenarios × 3 policies execute through the same engine and each result survives an exact Parquet round-trip.

EVIDENCE / H

Defensive contracts

Scenario, rollout, policy, metric, and manifest boundaries validate shapes, ranges, and provenance; engine-owned copies isolate policy mutation.

Current truth boundary

What we do not know—yet.

  • No M3 scorecards. Collision, off-road, kinematic realism, and aggregate policy comparisons have not been implemented or measured.
  • No M5 counterfactual result. M2 keeps ego on its logged trajectory; ego perturbation and reactivity metrics remain planned.
  • No W1 real-data claim. WOMD ingestion and Waymax/JAX cloud validation are planned, not represented as completed work.
  • Limited nonlinear world-agent coverage. Current simulated curvature is mainly in merge; the left-turn family’s turning actor is the logged ego.

Next falsifiable question / M3Can independent kinematic, interaction, and map-adherence metrics expose CV’s geometric drift and IDM’s longitudinal response without hiding those differences inside a composite score? Counterfactual nonreactivity remains an M5 question.
